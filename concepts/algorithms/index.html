Concept 018

Algorithms

I · Origins
一1 · MARK三3 · ALGORITHM六6 · COMPUTE
一

1 · MARK

MesopotamiaEgypt/Eastern Mediterranean

An algorithm is a recipe that always works the same way when followed carefully. Babylonian clay tablets prescribe step-by-step solutions for survey and accounting problems; Egyptian papyri do likewise for rations, slopes, and bread. The “recipe tablet” idea is older than the Arabic-rooted word we now use. Early algorithms lived as worked examples a scribe could imitate, not as modern pseudocode—but the discipline of ordered, shareable steps was already a civic technology. A recipe you cannot teach to another scribe is only a private trick; algorithms begin when steps become communal.

三

3 · ALGORITHM

Baghdad/Islamic-PersianIndiaChina

The word algorithm traces to al-Khwārizmī (9th century, working in the Baghdad scholarly orbit), whose arithmetic and algebra texts taught procedures as public knowledge that could cross languages. Indian and Chinese traditions independently cultivated highly procedural mathematics—rod calculus, root extraction, and simultaneous systems such as fangcheng. Naming and circulating recipes made method a shared good: a traveler could teach a calculation in a new city without reinventing every step from scratch. Translation movements carried those recipes across scripts, proving that method can migrate even when spoken language differs.

六

6 · COMPUTE

American/Global ComputationalBritish Industrial/Engineering

From Ada Lovelace’s notes on Babbage’s Analytical Engine to compilers, operating systems, and complexity theory, algorithms became things machines execute and mathematicians analyze for time and correctness. Punch cards, stored programs, and proof assistants all inherit the tablet’s demand: say the steps so clearly that another agent—human or silicon—can run them and get the same result. The ALGORITHM epoch’s recipes found their ultimate apprentice in the computer. Sorting, searching, and encryption routines are contemporary recipe tablets written for processors instead of apprentices.
