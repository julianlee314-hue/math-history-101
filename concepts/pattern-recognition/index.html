Concept 008

Pattern Recognition

I · Origins

Humans spotted patterns in notches, seasons, and craft long before school arithmetic. Some readings of the Ishango bone (c. 20,000–25,000 years ago) see grouped marks that look like more than raw tallies—though that reading stays debated. By the Old Babylonian period (early second millennium BCE), clay tablets show deliberate numerical sequences and tables. Pattern recognition is the capacity that turns “one, two, three…” into “what comes next?” A series chain in Mathera is a small, tangible version of that ancient habit: notice the rule, predict the next link, and feel mathematics as rhythm rather than rote.
