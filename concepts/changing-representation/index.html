Concept 035

Changing Representation

I · Origins
一1 · MARK四4 · PRINT
一

1 · MARK

MesopotamiaEgypt/Eastern Mediterranean

The same quantity can wear many costumes: tokens, tallies, words, digits. Mesopotamian accounting that moved from tokens to impressed marks already practiced changing representation. The point is fidelity—keep the meaning while swapping the medium so a new audience or tool can use it.

Moving a count from notches to tokens to seal impressions was already a politics of representation: each medium invited different frauds and different audits. The MARK epoch’s gift was making the idea durable in matter someone else could inspect later.

四

4 · PRINT

Italian Commercial/RenaissanceAtlantic Scientific Revolution

Algebraic symbolism, diagrams, and later multiple classroom representations (tables, graphs, equations) made switching forms a taught strategy. Flip-cards dramatize the move: same idea, new face. Print culture spread the belief that choosing a better representation is itself a mathematical act.

Reform movements in mathematics education elevated “multiple representations” as a right of every learner—print posters showing table, graph, and equation side by side. The PRINT epoch’s gift was multiplying the idea until classrooms and workshops shared one vocabulary.
