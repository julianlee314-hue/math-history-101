Concept 005

Place Value

I · Origins

Place value is the idea that where a digit sits changes what it means—units, tens, sixties. Mesopotamia developed a sexagesimal (base-60) positional system by around 2000 BCE; Chinese counting rods gave East Asia a decimal place-value board from roughly the first millennium BCE. The Hindu–Arabic system, with a true zero placeholder, matured in India and spread west through the Islamic world by the 8th–9th centuries CE. Europe’s slow switch from Roman numerals followed. A coin strip or grouped tallies rehearses the same insight: ten ones become one ten, and position does the heavy lifting that symbols alone once struggled to do.
