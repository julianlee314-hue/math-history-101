Concept 046

Geometric Transformation

II · Classical
二2 · WRITE五5 · MECHANIZE
二

2 · WRITE

Greek/Hellenistic

Moving a figure without tearing it—sliding, turning—was implicit in Greek superposition arguments. Transformations are the verbs of geometry: what you do to a shape. Writing about constructions already choreographed those moves even when the word “transformation” waited centuries.

Constructions that copy a segment or erect a perpendicular are transformation recipes written as ritual steps with compass and straightedge. The WRITE epoch’s gift was making the idea arguable in sentences and diagrams strangers could check. 五

5 · MECHANIZE

Central Europe 19th-c AbstractAtlantic Scientific Revolution

Analytic formulas for rotations and projections, pantographs, and optical instruments made transformations calculable and buildable. Klein’s insight—geometry as the study of invariants under transformation groups—reframed the field. Transform lenses in teaching echo the idea: change the view, track what survives.

Computer graphics and robotic motion planning later inherited the same verbs—translate, rotate, project—now executed by matrices at frame rate. The MECHANIZE epoch’s gift was embodying the idea in instruments that enforced it with gears and scales.
