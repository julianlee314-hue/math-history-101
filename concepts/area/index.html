Concept 023

Area

I · Origins
一1 · MARK二2 · WRITE五5 · MECHANIZE
一

1 · MARK

Egypt/Eastern MediterraneanMesopotamia

Area began as field and floor—how much land after the flood, how many bricks for a courtyard. Egyptian and Babylonian texts compute areas of rectangles, triangles, and trapezoids with practical rules for scribes and surveyors. Exactness varied; some Egyptian circle procedures are approximate. Fair shares of land and labor need numbers for surfaces. When taxes depend on field area, a wrong rule is a political injury.

二

2 · WRITE

Greek/Hellenistic

Euclid and Archimedes made area a matter of proof: congruence arguments, method of exhaustion, and careful comparisons that did not rely on a memorized formula sheet. Area became something you could argue in a diagram, not only survey with a rope. Greek writing turned the farmer’s question into a theorem—same practical question, stricter answer. Manuscript Elements carried those arguments into distant classrooms.

五

5 · MECHANIZE

Atlantic Scientific RevolutionBritish Industrial/Engineering

Planimeters traced irregular boundaries and read area off a dial—measurement without tiling by hand with unit squares. Analytic geometry and integral calculus treated area as an accumulated product for engineering drawings. Area-sweep instruments and computed integrals kept the ancient field problem alive on the drafting table. Shipbuilders needed tools that worked on curved outlines, not only neat rectangles. Factories and laboratories needed surface measure as urgently as temples once did.
