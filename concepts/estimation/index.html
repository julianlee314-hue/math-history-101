Concept 016

Estimation

I · Origins
一1 · MARK五5 · MECHANIZE
一

1 · MARK

Egypt/Eastern MediterraneanMesopotamia

Estimation is knowing when “about” is enough—herd size at a glance, grain in a bin, days left on a journey. Farmers, captains, and market sellers practiced it long before schoolbooks named the skill. An estimate stick or rough tally accepts that some decisions cannot wait for a perfect count under perfect light. Estimation is judgment under time pressure, not a failed measurement. Civilizations that could not estimate poorly starved or sailed into storms. Good estimators were trusted community resources—people who could glance and rarely embarrass the harvest plan.

五

5 · MECHANIZE

Atlantic Scientific RevolutionBritish Industrial/Engineering

Scientific estimation grew explicit rules: order-of-magnitude thinking, significant digits, and propagated error from instrument to result. Engineers designing beams and gunners ranging shot needed good-enough numbers with known risk—estimation with a warranty rather than a shrug. Instrument culture made “roughly” respectable when paired with stated bounds. The estimate stick graduated into Fermi problems, tolerance stacks, and back-of-envelope design that still saves projects before finer calculation begins. Slide rules and nomograms made rapid estimation a trained professional habit, not only folk wisdom. The MECHANIZE epoch’s gift was embodying the idea in instruments that enforced it with gears and scales.
