Concept 016

Estimation

I · Origins
一1 · MARK五5 · MECHANIZE
一

1 · MARK

Egypt/Eastern MediterraneanMesopotamia

Estimation is knowing when “about” is enough—herd size at a glance, grain in a bin, days left on a journey. Farmers, captains, and market sellers practiced it long before schoolbooks named the skill. An estimate stick accepts that some decisions cannot wait for a perfect count. Estimation is judgment under time pressure, not failed measurement. Civilizations that estimated poorly starved or sailed into storms.

五

5 · MECHANIZE

Atlantic Scientific RevolutionBritish Industrial/Engineering

Scientific estimation grew explicit rules: order-of-magnitude thinking, significant digits, and propagated error. Engineers and gunners needed good-enough numbers with known risk—estimation with a warranty. Instrument culture made “roughly” respectable when paired with stated bounds. Slide rules and nomograms made rapid estimation a trained habit, not only folk wisdom. Back-of-envelope design still saves projects before finer calculation begins.
