Concept 010

Shape

I · Origins
一1 · MARK二2 · WRITE
一

1 · MARK

Egypt/Eastern MediterraneanMesopotamia

Shape is classification grown precise: not just “alike,” but circle, triangle, rectangle with shared properties. Egyptian rope stretchers (harpedonaptai) laid out foundations and fields with knotted cords from early dynastic times (from about 3000 BCE), and tomb scenes show survey teams at work after the Nile’s flood. Babylonian school texts computed areas of familiar figures. Greeks later named and proved; Egyptians and Mesopotamians mostly used. Mathera’s shape tiles keep the practical spirit: handle the form first, name its features, and only then treat geometry as a language rather than a mystery.

二

2 · WRITE

Greek/Hellenistic

With compass and straightedge, Greek geometers turned shapes into argued objects—definitions, postulates, theorems you could follow without standing in a field. Euclid’s Elements (c. 300 BCE) made the triangle and the circle the public language of exact form for two millennia of readers. Diagrams on papyrus and parchment let a student reconstruct a figure step by step. Shape became something you could win an argument with in ink, not only something you could pave with stone. The WRITE epoch is where form learned to speak in proofs.
