Concept 002

Cardinality

I · Origins

Cardinality is the quiet leap from marking to knowing: the last number you say names the whole set. Shepherds who could not write still used tally sticks this way for centuries—match mark to sheep, then trust the final count. By the Neolithic Near East (roughly 8000–3000 BCE), clay tokens sorted by shape did similar work for commodities. A modern ten-frame is a small stage for the same idea: fill the frame until the last cell tells you “ten,” not ten separate ones. Once children grasp cardinality, counting stops being a chant and becomes an answer.
