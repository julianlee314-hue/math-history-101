Reading Graphs

III · Medieval
四4 · PRINT六6 · COMPUTE
四

4 · PRINT

British Industrial/EngineeringAtlantic Scientific Revolution

Once graphs proliferated, literacy had to include reading them: axes, scales, slope stories, misleading truncations. Nineteenth-century statistical graphics and school curricula trained publics to interpret plotted quantities. Reading graphs is civic defense against persuasive pictures that lie with true ink.

Florence Nightingale’s polar area diagrams and other reform graphics showed that reading graphs could change public policy, not only exam scores. The PRINT epoch’s gift was multiplying the idea until classrooms and workshops shared one vocabulary. 6 · COMPUTE

American/Global Computational

Dashboards and interactive charts demand the same skepticism at higher speed. Software can rescale, filter, and animate; readers must still ask what was left out. Graph-reading skill matured from paper plots to glowing screens without retiring the old questions about scale and cherry-picked ranges.

Hover tooltips and filters help, yet they can also hide the raw distribution; careful readers still download the table behind the pretty picture. The COMPUTE epoch’s gift was letting machines execute the idea faster than any room of clerks.
