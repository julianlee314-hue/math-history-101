Concept 033

Tables

I · Origins
一1 · MARK三3 · ALGORITHM四4 · PRINT
一

1 · MARK

MesopotamiaEgypt/Eastern Mediterranean

Tables store answers before you need them—reciprocals, products, square roots, rations. Old Babylonian mathematical tablets are famous for dense numerical tables that powered scribal work. A table is a bargain with the future: compute once, look up many times. If the reciprocal table is wrong, every division that leans on it inherits the lie—so scribal schools guarded tables carefully.

三

3 · ALGORITHM

Baghdad/Islamic-PersianIndiaChina

Astronomical and trigonometric tables in Indian, Islamic, and Chinese traditions made prediction procedural: find the row, interpolate if needed, apply the rule. Manuscript tables were scientific infrastructure as real as observatories. Interpolation between tabulated values turned sparse tables into continuous tools—algorithm filling gaps the scribe did not compute by hand.

四

4 · PRINT

Atlantic Scientific RevolutionBritish Industrial/Engineering

Printed logarithm tables and nautical almanacs multiplied table culture globally. Navigation, engineering, and insurance depended on pages of precomputed values. Napier and Briggs’s logarithms shaved months from astronomical and navigational labor; ships quite literally sailed on tables. Even after electronic calculation, the idea survives in spreadsheets: organized lookup beats recomputing from scratch. Organized lookup still beats recomputing from scratch—now in spreadsheets and databases.
