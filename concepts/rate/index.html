Concept 064

Rate

I · Origins
一1 · MARK五5 · MECHANIZE
一

1 · MARK

MesopotamiaEgypt/Eastern Mediterranean

Rate is change per unit—distance per day, work per worker, interest per season. Hourglasses and travel logs made rates tangible. Early accounts already asked how fast surplus grows or food dwindles. Rate is timeful arithmetic: not only how much, but how quickly. Marks that track change over time let later workers audit the pace of a journey or a debt. Early accounts asked how fast surplus grows or food dwindles—timeful arithmetic.

五

5 · MECHANIZE

Atlantic Scientific RevolutionBritish Industrial/Engineering

Velocity, fluxions, and industrial throughput put rates at the center of science and factories. Gauges and tachometers displayed rate on dials. Calculus named instantaneous rate; engines demanded average rates you could fuel. Instruments made “how fast” a reading you could contract for, not only a traveller’s guess. Rate left the travel log and entered the factory floor.
