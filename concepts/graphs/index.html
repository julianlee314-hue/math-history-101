Graphs

III · Medieval

三

3 · ALGORITHM

Italian Commercial/RenaissanceBaghdad/Islamic-Persian

Before modern graphs, scholars plotted intensities and latitudes of forms—Nicole Oresme’s 14th-century configurations are a famous medieval step toward graphing change. Aligning magnitude with a drawn length prepared later coordinate pictures. Oresme’s figurative latitudes hinted that intensity over time could be drawn—an intellectual permission slip for later scientists to plot. Graphs begin when comparison wants a shape, not only a number.

四

4 · PRINT

Atlantic Scientific RevolutionBritish Industrial/Engineering

Analytic geometry and cheap graph paper made plotted curves common in science and industry. Playfair’s statistical charts and engineering plots taught publics to read pictures of data. Railway timetables and factory output charts taught managers to manage by eye. Printed graphs turned invisible trends into visible slopes anyone in the room could argue about.

六

6 · COMPUTE

American/Global Computational

Interactive plotting software and dashboards made graphs the default face of data. Computation draws millions of points without fatigue, but the read skill remains human: what story does the pixel claim? Default chart generators tempt misuse—3D pies and truncated axes—so computational abundance raised the stakes of graph literacy. Scatter boards and screens share the same demand: read the claim, not only the ink.
