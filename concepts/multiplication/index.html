Concept 058

Multiplication

I · Origins
一1 · MARK三3 · ALGORITHM六6 · COMPUTE
一

1 · MARK

Egypt/Eastern MediterraneanMesopotamia

Multiplication began as repeated joining—Egyptian doubling, Babylonian tables, areas of rectangles. It answered “how many in all?” when groups of equal size stacked. Tables and strips made the operation look-uppable. Trade scaled faster once multiplication left the slow lane of adding one by one. Marks and tables let later workers check the product without trusting a single memory.

三

3 · ALGORITHM

ChinaIndiaBaghdad/Islamic-Persian

Lattice methods, rod calculus, and Hindu–Arabic algorithms turned multiplication into compact column procedures. Islamic and European merchants adopted faster hands. Classroom grid methods still echo those board techniques. Named steps let apprentices repeat the method without reinventing each motion—multiplication as shared craft. Faster column methods turned piled groups into a clerk skill you could audit.

六

6 · COMPUTE

American/Global Computational

Hardware multipliers and big-integer libraries made multiplication a primitive of computation. Cryptography and graphics spend fortunes of cycles multiplying. The ancient “groups of” question now runs in silicon pipelines. What once filled merchant boards now finishes in nanoseconds inside every chip. Multiplication left the merchant board and entered every arithmetic unit.
