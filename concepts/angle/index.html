Concept 041

Angle

I · Origins
一1 · MARK二2 · WRITE
一

1 · MARK

Egypt/Eastern MediterraneanMesopotamia

Angles began as corners in building and sighting—walls meeting, slopes of ramps, wedges of land. Egyptian seked problems and Babylonian degree-like divisions of the circle show early quantitative grip on turn and corner. A sector wedge makes the idea graspable: a slice of turn you can hold.

Military ramps and pyramid slopes made angle a matter of labor cost: too steep, and hauling fails; too shallow, and the project sprawls. The MARK epoch’s gift was making the idea durable in matter someone else could inspect later.

二

2 · WRITE

Greek/HellenisticBaghdad/Islamic-Persian

Greek geometry defined angle carefully and built congruence and parallel theory upon it; Hellenistic and Islamic astronomy refined degree measure for the sky. Written angle measure let distant observers share a turn without sharing a hillside. Protractors and inscribed degrees are the everyday heirs of that agreement.

Degree, minute, and second inherited sexagesimal habits from astronomy, a Mesopotamian ghost still living in every protractor sold today. The WRITE epoch’s gift was making the idea arguable in sentences and diagrams strangers could check.
