Concept 030

Scaling

I · Origins
一1 · MARK五5 · MECHANIZE
一

1 · MARK

Egypt/Eastern MediterraneanMesopotamia

Scaling is resizing while keeping form’s relations—models of temples, map reductions, recipe doublings. Builders who enlarged a plan by ropes and modules practiced similarity before the word existed. Scaling lets a small trustworthy model stand in for a costly original, which is why every ambitious building culture invented it early.

Architects who enlarged a clay model by modular rods were staking lives on scaling: a small error in the factor became a catastrophic error in the wall. The MARK epoch’s gift was making the idea durable in matter someone else could inspect later.

五

5 · MECHANIZE

Atlantic Scientific RevolutionBritish Industrial/Engineering

Pantographs, proportional compasses, and engineered drawings made scaling a desk instrument. Mapmaking empires and machine shops alike needed faithful enlargements and reductions. Similarity theory from Greek geometry met industrial need: scale factors became stamped on blueprints, and mistakes in scaling became expensive in steel.

Ordnance survey maps and machine-tool drawings stamped scale ratios in print so distant workshops could enlarge the same idea without meeting the designer. The MECHANIZE epoch’s gift was embodying the idea in instruments that enforced it with gears and scales.
