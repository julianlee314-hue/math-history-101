Concept 039

Periodicity

I · Origins
一1 · MARK五5 · MECHANIZE
一

1 · MARK

Egypt/Eastern MediterraneanMesopotamiaChina

Periodicity is the return—the flood season, the moon’s phases, market weeks, ritual calendars. Early astronomers and farmers marked cycles because survival tracked repetition. Clocks and sundials later made smaller periods visible hour by hour. To notice a period is to predict the next occurrence before it arrives.

Lunisolar calendars are negotiated compromises between incommensurable periods—periodicity politics written into festival lists. The MARK epoch’s gift was making the idea durable in matter someone else could inspect later. The MARK epoch’s gift was making the idea durable in matter someone else could inspect later.

五

5 · MECHANIZE

Atlantic Scientific RevolutionFrench Enlightenment/Revolutionary

Pendulums, geared clocks, and wave studies turned periodicity into measurable frequency. Fourier analysis (nineteenth century) then treated complex motions as sums of periodic pieces. Mechanized timekeeping and analytic wave theory made “again and again” a laboratory object with units.

Harrison’s chronometers made a portable period (the second) accurate enough to solve longitude, tying periodicity to empire and survival at sea. The MECHANIZE epoch’s gift was embodying the idea in instruments that enforced it with gears and scales.
