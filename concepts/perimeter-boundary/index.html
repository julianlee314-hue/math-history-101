Concept 043

Perimeter / Boundary

I · Origins
一1 · MARK二2 · WRITE
一

1 · MARK

Egypt/Eastern MediterraneanMesopotamia

Perimeter is the edge length of a field or wall—the ribbon around a region. Survey after flood, fence planning, and brick counts needed the boundary measure as much as the interior. City walls and property ditches made perimeter a security and legal measure; length of boundary could matter more than area in a dispute. Borders are political; perimeters make them numeric.

二

2 · WRITE

Greek/Hellenistic

Greek geometry related perimeter to figures systematically—polygons, circles, and isoperimetric questions about which shape encloses most for a given edge. Writing turned the fence problem into theorems. The boundary became a mathematical object with properties, not only a pile of fencing posts. Isoperimetric problems linked the fence question to variational thinking still alive in analysis. The fence problem became theorems with properties, portable across readers who never saw the field.
