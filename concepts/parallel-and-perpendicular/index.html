Parallel & Perpendicular

I · Origins

一

1 · MARK

Egypt/Eastern MediterraneanMesopotamia

Builders needed lines that never meet and lines that meet square. Rope stretchers and set squares made perpendicular a craft fact; irrigation canals and wall courses demanded parallels you could trust. Rail-square tools still teach that right angle by feel.

Irrigation grids that failed to keep ditches parallel wasted water and authority; parallelism was hydraulic statecraft. The MARK epoch’s gift was making the idea durable in matter someone else could inspect later. The MARK epoch’s gift was making the idea durable in matter someone else could inspect later.

二

2 · WRITE

Greek/Hellenistic

Euclid’s parallel postulate made parallelism a foundational—and later controversial—axiom. Perpendicularity structured congruence proofs and area arguments. When nineteenth-century geometers explored alternatives, they showed how much of classical geometry hung on that one parallel idea. Writing turned a builder’s habit into a philosophical hinge.

Saccheri, Gauss, Bolyai, and Lobachevsky’s explorations of parallels showed that geometry’s “obvious” lines could fork into new worlds—written thought experiments with revolutionary consequences. The WRITE epoch’s gift was making the idea arguable in sentences and diagrams strangers could check.
