Symbolic Representation

IV · Renaissance

四

4 · PRINT

Italian Commercial/RenaissanceAtlantic Scientific Revolution

Symbolic algebra—letters for unknowns and givens—crystallized in the work of Viète, Descartes, and their print-era peers. Symbols compressed rhetoric into manipulable marks. Formula cards and algebraic notation let arguments become calculations. When symbols stabilize in type, mathematics gains a second spoken language made of ink.

Syncopated algebra gave way to fully symbolic forms that printers could set consistently; notation wars slowly settled into the symbols classrooms still use. The PRINT epoch’s gift was multiplying the idea until classrooms and workshops shared one vocabulary.

6 · COMPUTE

American/Global Computational

Computer algebra systems manipulate symbols as data, inheriting the print-era dream that marks can be transformed by rules alone. Symbolic representation now runs in silicon as well as on paper, but it still depends on human choices about what the letters mean.

CAS tools can rearrange expressions humans find tedious, but they still require a human who understands which symbolic form answers the question asked.
