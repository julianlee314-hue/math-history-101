Linear Combination & Span

VI · Industrial

六

6 · COMPUTE

Central Europe 19th-c AbstractAmerican/Global Computational

Linear combinations mix vectors with scalar weights; their span is the reachable subspace. Nineteenth-century linear algebra named the idea; computation uses it constantly. Basis frames show generators whose mixes fill a space. Span answers “what can these building arrows reach?”—now checked by matrix rank at machine speed. Rank checks ask, at machine speed, what the building arrows can actually reach.

7 · INTELLIGENCE

American/Global Computational

Representation learning asks which spanning sets make tasks easy. Feature spans and dictionary learning are span talk in AI clothing. Intelligence searches useful combinations; linear algebra states what combinations can mean. Useful bases are learned; what they can reach is still span theory.
