Twelve Worlds

Signature civilizations from the History Network—places that especially progressed or proliferated mathematical ideas, instruments, and institutions.

01

Mesopotamia

Clay-tablet computation

From late Neolithic tokens to Old Babylonian school tablets, Mesopotamia made quantity durable in clay. Sexagesimal place-value habits, reciprocal and multiplication tables, and worked survey problems turned accounting into a teachable craft. Ideas that especially proliferated here include positional calculation, numerical tables, and algorithmic word problems.

Artefacts: Clay tokens, Tally stick, Manuscript table, Recipe tablet

02

Egypt/Eastern Mediterranean

Survey, measure, record

Egyptian mathematics shines in measurement and practical arithmetic—cubit rods, rope stretchers after the Nile flood, unit-fraction recipes, and doubling methods on papyrus. The Eastern Mediterranean corridor carried survey, trade weights, and later Hellenistic astronomy. Techs especially progressed: units, area/volume rules, and recorded measurement culture.

Artefacts: Ruler, Shape tiles, Pan balance, Fraction bar

03

Greek/Hellenistic

Proof and geometric structure

Greek and Hellenistic cultures made proof and diagram the public language of mathematics. Euclid, Archimedes, Apollonius, and astronomical chord tables set templates for definition, postulate, and theorem. Ideas that traveled farthest from here include Euclidean geometry, rigorous proportion, loci, and the ideal of demonstrative argument.

Artefacts: Compass & straightedge, Elements scroll, Proof seal, Locus string

04

China

Algorithmic state mathematics

Chinese mathematics emphasized procedural power for a bureaucratic state: counting rods, the Nine Chapters tradition, fangcheng elimination, and root extraction algorithms. Decimal place-value on boards and practical geometry for earthworks flourished. Especially progressed: algorithmic linear systems, rod calculus, and state-facing problem collections.

Artefacts: Counting rods, Abacus, Twin scale, Volume jar

05

India

Numeral and computational abstraction

Indian mathematics matured place-value decimal numerals with zero, refined trigonometry (sines), and advanced algebra and infinite series in several historical layers. Combinatorial counting and computational verse traditions supported both ritual and astronomy. Ideas that proliferated globally include Hindu–Arabic numerals and powerful root/equation procedures.

Artefacts: Decimal ruler, Sine plate, Power tower, Outcome tree

06

Baghdad/Islamic-Persian

Translation → algebra → synthesis

In the Baghdad orbit and wider Islamic-Persian world, translation movements met original synthesis: al-Khwārizmī’s algebra and arithmetic, astronomical tables, and refined trigonometry. Algorithms became teachable across languages. Especially progressed: named algebra, propagated numeral methods, and table-rich scientific computation.

Artefacts: Recipe tablet, Astrolabe, Manuscript table, Algebra tiles

07

Italian Commercial/Renaissance

Merchant math becomes print math

Italian abacus schools and merchant manuals turned calculation into urban craft, then print multiplied arithmetic and early symbolic algebra across Europe. Pacioli-era bookkeeping and equation contests made math a public spectacle of skill. Especially proliferated: commercial arithmetic, percent/interest culture, and printed problem books.

Artefacts: Merchant ledger, Printed arithmetic, Percent wheel, Solve balance

08

Atlantic Scientific Revolution

Instrument + experiment + symbolic mathematics

From the seventeenth century Atlantic world, instruments, experiment, and symbolic mathematics braided together—analytic geometry, calculus, probability’s early letters, and precision devices. Nature became writable as equations checked by brass and glass. Especially progressed: functions, rates, limits-in-practice, and scientific notation culture.

Artefacts: Telescope, Pendulum, Tangent glass, Graph paper

09

French Enlightenment/Revolutionary

Mathematics becomes an institution

French Enlightenment and Revolutionary cultures institutionalized mathematics—metric reform, École networks, probabilistic social science, and ambitious analytic mechanics. Units and curricula became state projects. Especially progressed: metric standardization, institutional teaching, and probability as civic reasoning.

Artefacts: Measuring cup, Expectation purse, Bell jar, Peak dial

10

Central Europe 19th-c Abstract

Rigor and structure

Nineteenth-century Central European mathematics rebuilt foundations—ε–δ rigor, group and linear-algebraic structure, set-theoretic language, and abstract definitions that travel. Proof standards tightened as applications exploded elsewhere. Especially progressed: analysis rigor, algebraic structure, matrices/eigenideas, and modern axiomatic style.

Artefacts: Limit funnel, Continuity ribbon, Matrix press, Eigen-axis

11

British Industrial/Engineering

Mathematics as infrastructure

British industrial and engineering cultures made mathematics infrastructure—gauges, tables, statistics for populations and factories, slide rules, and surveying empires. Tolerances and schedules ruled. Especially progressed: applied statistics, precision measurement, logarithmic computation tools, and engineering drawing standards.

Artefacts: Slide rule, Vernier caliper, Logarithm table, Spread whiskers

12

American/Global Computational

Electronics → software → AI

American and global computational cultures turned algorithms into electronics, software, and AI—punch cards to GPUs, spreadsheets to proof assistants. Mathematics increasingly executes and searches itself. Especially progressed: programmable algorithms, numerical linear algebra, statistical learning, and machine-checked proof.

Artefacts: Punch card, Spreadsheet grid, Proof assistant, Logic gate

Proliferation map

Mesopotamia and Egypt/Eastern Mediterranean pushed MARK-era storage, units, and survey arithmetic; China and India deepened ALGORITHM-era place-value calculation and procedural solving; Greek/Hellenistic culture made WRITE-era proof contagious; Baghdad/Islamic-Persian networks synthesized algebra and tables for export; Italian Commercial/Renaissance print turned merchant methods into Europe-wide school math; the Atlantic Scientific Revolution and French institutional reforms mechanized and standardized scientific mathematics; Central European abstraction and British engineering infrastructure prepared COMPUTE-scale application; American/Global Computational culture now proliferates execution, data, and INTELLIGENCE-era search across all prior ideas.
